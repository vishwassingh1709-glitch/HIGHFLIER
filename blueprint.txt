1. Project vision

HIGHFLIER will be an independent Indian e-commerce storefront. Customers browse products, add them to cart,
checkout and pay on HIGHFLIER. Products may be sourced from one or more Indian suppliers. When a supplier
officially supports the required API or approved feed, HIGHFLIER can sync product data and stock, submit eligible
orders to that supplier, and sync shipment tracking back to the customer account.
This is a supplier-fulfilled / dropshipping-style model. It is not simply copying another website.

Core customer journey

1 Supplier shares product catalogue through an authorized API, product feed, or approved integration.
2 HIGHFLIER imports mapped product data: supplier SKU/ID, title, description, images, price, stock and category.
3 HIGHFLIER applies its own selling-price rules and displays products on its storefront.
4 Customer places an order and completes payment (or selects COD only if the fulfilment arrangement supports it).
5 Backend verifies payment, validates current price and stock, records the order, and submits it to the supplier.
6 Supplier accepts the order, packs and dispatches it, and returns tracking/status information.
7 HIGHFLIER updates the customer's order page and sends appropriate notifications.
Business margin example: Supplier cost s500; HIGHFLIER selling price s799; indicative gross difference s299.
This is not net profit: shipping, payment fees, GST/tax, returns, RTO, discounts and other costs must be
accounted for.

3. Supplier integration: the most important requirement

HIGHFLIER must not depend on scraping or copying a supplier website without permission. A URL alone is not
enough to import products or place orders. The supplier must provide an authorized API, documented product
feed, approved integration, or written permission and technical method for catalogue and fulfilment operations.

Supplier capability checklist

q Product catalogue API/feed: product ID, SKU, title, description, category, images, variants and attributes.
q Pricing API/feed: supplier cost, currency, price changes and any applicable fees.
q Inventory API/feed: available stock and last-updated time.
q Order placement API: create an order with customer shipping details and supplier SKU/variant.
q Order status API or webhook: accepted, processing, shipped, delivered, cancelled, etc.
q Tracking API/webhook: courier, tracking number and tracking URL.
q Returns/cancellation/refund policy and API support, if available.
q India delivery coverage, PIN-code serviceability, shipping fees and estimated delivery.
q Dropshipping / blind-shipping / white-label packaging terms, if HIGHFLIER branding is desired.
q Commercial terms: supplier cost, settlement, GST invoices, COD availability, RTO and return liability.

Supplier onboarding strategy

Build a supplier-agnostic integration layer. Start with one supplier that explicitly supports custom-website API access.
Add further suppliers only after the first integration works end-to-end. Potential research leads mentioned in planning
include FreshOn (grocery-focused partner API), Qikink (print-on-demand/apparel fulfilment) and Shiprocket
(shipping/logistics, not a general product supplier). Their current API access, eligibility and commercial terms must be
checked directly; do not assume they offer a universal multi-category catalogue.

Supplier adapter design

Create a common adapter contract with methods such as testConnection(), fetchProducts(), fetchInventory(),
createSupplierOrder(), getOrderStatus(), getTrackingDetails() and cancelSupplierOrder(). Each supplier gets its own
adapter implementation. Do not assume all suppliers expose the same endpoints or payload formats.

8. Critical order, payment and inventory rules

1 Never trust product price, discount, stock, shipping fee or coupon totals sent by the browser. Recalculate on the
     backend.

2 For online payment, create the payment order server-side and verify the provider signature/payment status
     server-side. Use verified webhooks and idempotency.

3 Do not mark an order as paid based only on a frontend success page.

4 Reserve or decrement inventory safely. Prevent overselling with atomic conditional updates or transactions, and
     define reservation expiry/rollback.

5 Only submit supplier orders according to the agreed payment/fulfilment policy. Record the supplier request and
     response safely.

6 Use an idempotency key or unique external reference for supplier order submission. Retry transient failures
     without creating duplicate supplier orders.

7 Keep customer order status and supplier fulfilment status separate, then map them through explicit allowed state
     transitions.

8 If supplier submission fails after customer payment, keep the paid order visible, mark fulfilment as action-required,
     retry safely and alert admin. Define cancellation/refund handling.

9. Security, compliance and reliability

q Hash passwords with bcryptjs and use secure JWT access/refresh token handling.
q Keep supplier API keys and payment secrets server-side; encrypt credentials at rest and restrict admin access.
q Validate supplier base URLs against approved HTTPS domains to prevent SSRF. Do not let arbitrary users

     submit URLs for backend fetching.
q Use Helmet, CORS allowlists, rate limiting, request validation, safe error messages and structured logging.
q Verify webhook signatures; deduplicate webhook events and supplier callbacks.
q Use pagination and query limits; validate ObjectIds and prevent mass assignment.
q Use audit logs for supplier connection changes, pricing rules, refunds and admin actions.
q Confirm supplier permission for product text/images, resale, pricing, branding, customer-data transfer and

     fulfilment.
q Plan Indian GST invoicing, consumer disclosures, privacy/data handling, refunds, returns and applicable legal/tax

     advice before launch.
